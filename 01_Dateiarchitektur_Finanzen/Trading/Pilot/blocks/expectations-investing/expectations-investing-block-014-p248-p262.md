9. Companies and investors often incorrectly associate the “return” from
a buyback with accounting-based measures, such as the inverse of the P/E
multiple. The (faulty) logic is as follows: Say the consensus expects a company
to earn $1 per share. The shares trade at $25, a P/E ratio of 25. So the company gets $1 in earnings for every $25 share it buys back for a “return” of
4 percent (1/25). The ﬂaw is that investors cannot reliably link a P/E multiple to
the cost of equity because multiples are a shorthand that incorporate variables
other than the discount rate. These variables include sales growth, operating
margins, investment needs, and the sustainability of competitive advantage.
